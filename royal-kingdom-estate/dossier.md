# Royal Kingdom Estate: prospect dossier

29 Sep 2026 · https://www.royalkingdomestate.com · researched by Claude with the prospect-research skill

## Verdict: Go
Royal Kingdom sells $30,000–$99,000 land and homes, mostly to Ghanaians abroad (they toured five US cities in Oct 2025 to find them). Yet every enquiry goes through a six-field form, and the project pages don't answer what a buyer abroad asks before paying anything: payment plan, plot size, documents, completion date. **Approach them with a buyer concierge that answers those questions at any hour in the buyer's time zone, qualifies them, and books a site visit or video call for the sales team.** One plot sold is worth $30,000.

  - Homes: Grace City Prime Homes in Aburi ($99,000) and The Future apartments at East Airport ($79,000).

- **They earn from direct sales of plots and units**, plus services. Margins and payment plans are unknown. The site promises "flexible payment plans" but never states one.
- LinkedIn advertised "GH₵100k for Phase 1 plots (through September)". It's unclear whether this is Grace City, which the site lists at $30,000. Ask; don't claim a mismatch.

## What's broken (free fixes to open with)
0. **Their Google profile is categorised as "Industrial real estate agency".** Changing it to a residential real estate developer category is a two-minute edit that decides which searches they appear in.
1. **Their old website address is dead but still in Google.** Search results show royalkingdomestategh.com as "Royal Kingdom Estate - Home", but the domain no longer exists (checked on two public DNS servers). Anyone clicking that result gets an error.
2. **There's no WhatsApp button** on a site selling to Ghanaians, at home and abroad, who live on WhatsApp. Visitors get three phone numbers and a form.
3. **Project pages skip the questions buyers ask first:** payment plan terms, plot size, title and documents, completion date. The short-stay "$100" has no "per night" and no booking calendar.
4. **Prices mix currencies across the same land page** ($30,000, GH¢220,000, GH¢180,000), which makes comparison hard for a buyer abroad.
5. Small ones: the footer reads "Copyright © 2025 ." with a stray space, the /land tab title says "RKE Services", and "Similar properties" links to "#".
6. **Check on your phone before mentioning:** the homepage counters ("0+ Lands, 0+ Projects, 0+ Happy Clients") read 0 in the page source and in a background browser. They probably count up when scrolled into view on a real phone. If they stay at 0, that's the most visible fix of all.

**Raise privately, in person only:** the lapsed royalkingdomestategh.com can be registered by anyone. A scammer could put up a lookalike site and take deposits from diaspora buyers who find it through the old search result. Suggest they re-register it and redirect it to the current site.

## The pain to pitch
- **Primary problem: customers.** Enquiries from abroad don't turn into site visits and sales fast enough, because:
  - the target buyer is 4–8 hours behind Accra (the US tour);
  - the questions that decide a $30k purchase aren't answered on the page;
  - the only paths are a form, three phone numbers, or two shared inboxes.
- **Equal first: leads, meaning trust at the moment of checking.** Their marketing (US tour, 16K on Instagram) creates people who then ask Google or ChatGPT "is Royal Kingdom legit?". What they get back is a court case, a scam score and a similarly named fraud story, not 45 five-star reviews. And they're absent from every "who can I trust?" answer, where Devtraco and Regimanuel Gray are named. **Leads = people asking × share who see you on the shortlist (0 of 10 today) × share who trust what they read when they check.** Add the dead old domain, the missing WhatsApp button and the wrong Google category.
- **Money math:** one Grace City plot is $30,000 (about GH¢347,000). A 30-day pilot pays for itself if it moves **one** buyer from enquiry to a site visit and a sale, and that's before counting homes at $79k–$99k. *Assumption: we don't know their margin; ask how much a plot sale earns them after costs.*

## Demo angle
- **Data source:** the public pages (6 projects: price, location, amenities, descriptions) and 12 blog posts on documents, litigation-free land and land banking, copied into a small JSON file. There's no API to snapshot.
- **The two-minute demo is a buyer abroad at 11pm Atlanta time:**
  1. The buyer types "I'm in Atlanta with about $35k, I want land near Accra I can build on later."
  2. The concierge shows Grace City Prime Lands ($30,000, Aburi, 40 minutes from the airport, serviced) and The View.
  3. It answers "is the land litigation-free, what documents do I get?" from their own blog, with the source linked.
  4. Asked "what's the payment plan?", it says honestly that it needs the sales team, and logs this as a question the site can't answer. That turns the gap into a pitch point.
  5. It qualifies the buyer (cash or instalments, timeline, visiting Ghana or not) and books a video call slot in Atlanta time for sales.
  6. `/admin` shows the conversation, the qualified lead, and "questions buyers asked that your site can't answer".
- **Base:** the ProptisGH app (the prospect-demo skill), with projects instead of listings.

## Where Jev fits
- "Is this buyer ready for a sales call (named a project, a budget and a timeline)?" **Fits.** It's a yes/no judgement on the buyer's own words and gates the handoff.
- "Is the buyer asking something our pages don't answer (payment plan, documents, completion)?" **Fits.** It routes to a person and fills the knowledge-gap list.
- "Is the buyer's budget enough for this project?" **Doesn't fit.** That's arithmetic plus a currency conversion, so plain code.
- The video testimonials and site photos: **don't fit** without a text description first.

## Risks and reasons not to
- They're bigger than Proptis (51–200 staff, a CEO with a media profile). The CEO is hard to reach cold, so go through Seth Yamoah (marketing & innovation) or Caroline Lokko (customer relations).
- A Next.js site built in 2025 suggests a web agency or in-house developer who may feel protective. Position the concierge as plugging into their site, not replacing it.
- They may already run a CRM or call centre for the US leads. Ask before assuming there's no follow-up system.
- The office address differs between the site and LinkedIn. Confirm by phone.

## Questions only they can answer
1. When someone in the US fills the form at night, who replies and how soon?
2. How many enquiries a month, and what share come from abroad? What happened to the Bridge to Ghana leads?
3. What are the actual payment plans (deposit, instalments, term), and why aren't they on the site?
4. What does one plot sale earn after costs? What closes a sale: a site visit, a video call, a relative visiting?
5. Is the GH₵100k Phase 1 promo the same Grace City plot the site lists at $30,000?
6. Who decides on sales tools: Seth, Caroline or the COO?
