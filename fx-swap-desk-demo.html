3. The Three Deadly Traps & How Software Fixes Them

  • Trap 1: Triangular Fraud (The #1 scam in West Africa P2P)
    A scammer tricks an innocent third party in Lagos into paying the bureau's GTBank account, while the scammer claims the Cedis on a burner MoMo line in Ghana. Software fix: JEV compares deposit sender name, WhatsApp identity, and destination MoMo KYC. Name mismatches trigger mandatory ID verification.
  • Trap 2: The One-Way River (Float Depletion)
    On busy import days, 90% of requests are Naira to Cedis, draining the Ghana pool within 4 hours. Software fix: Dynamic Spread Engine automatically widens the rate as pool reserves drop below 25%, incentivizing reverse flows (GHS → NGN).
  • Trap 3: Bank Account Freezes
    Nigerian banks flag accounts with hundreds of miscellaneous P2P transfers. Software fix: Multi-Account Treasury Router rotates inflows across pre-approved corporate tier-3 accounts, enforcing strict unique alphanumeric references (no "crypto" or "fx" terms).
